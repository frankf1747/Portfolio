The problem

Every month, operations figures were transcribed from reports and pasted onto slides. The numbers arrived too late to act on, and the cause of a problem lived in someone's inbox.

What I built

Pipelines straight from the source systems into a lakehouse that people and agents can query, and automations that push a read and a next step to whoever can act on it.

Why it matters

The build is 85% faster, a monthly number became a weekly nudge, and the data underneath is now ready for agents to work on safely.

A dashboard answers the question we thought to build. Context answers the ones we didn't.

Automation 2 · training compliance

From a monthly number to a weekly nudge

Training and HR records meet in one semantic model. A weekly run does three jobs.

1 · Get the rate

On-time completion and average days late, 13 months by month.

2 · Find the causes

Overdue by manager, by learner and per course, with days overdue.

3 · Notify

Who is late, on what, and which due dates are coming up. Emailed to the learner and their manager.

Before

One monthly compliance rate. By the time anyone saw it, the due dates had passed.

Now

Weekly tracking and a targeted email to the people who can act, supporting a 95% on-time target.

Meaning · the context layer · 2

What the data was hiding

Before anything could be automated, it had to mean one thing. Three findings that changed what people did.

Blended metric

Most “shipments” never physically moved. Internal transfers sat in the same on-time rate as real cross-site shipments. Split, the result moved in the unflattering direction.

One code, two events

Two quality events shared one system code, and the system could not separate them. Proposed: change the code, or the definition.

Silent failure

Two key-padding conventions collapsed a join's match rate without raising an error. Now a test, not a surprise.

Agree the words before the numbers.
Confirm: do we include conditional release?
Confirm: which open majors does the rate leave out?
I recomputed the on-time metric under six definitions. The spread stayed narrow, so the number survives the argument.
